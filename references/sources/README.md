# Socrates Source Records

Five unique URLs are represented: three dialogue captures and two secondary
article excerpts. There are no transcripts or index-only records.

| Record | Actual coverage | Evidentiary use |
|---|---|---|
| [Plato: Apology](books/plato-apology.md) | English excerpt, cut during penalty discussion | Defense, ignorance, examination; final passages absent |
| [Plato: Crito](books/plato-crito.md) | English text through its final exchange | A particular argument about obligation and wrongdoing |
| [Plato: Phaedo](books/plato-phaedo.md) | English excerpt, cut during recollection discussion | Platonic argument and counterargument; not the complete work |
| [SEP: Socrates](articles/sep-socrates.md) | Excerpt ending within the source comparison | Secondary introduction to the Socratic problem |
| [Britannica: Socrates](articles/britannica-socrates.md) | Incomplete article excerpt | Secondary biographical and source orientation |

Crito has an ending marker but has not been collated against a critical edition.
Xenophon's works, Aristophanes' Clouds, and other Platonic dialogues discussed in
research are not captured locally. Further-reading mentions do not establish
passage-level verification. No ancient portrayal is a neutral trial transcript.

## Attribution and Reuse

Source text retains its original language. Keep editorial notes in English and
separate them from captured passages. Where the capture omits a byline, translator,
or revision date, mark it unknown rather than supplying a guess. Apology explicitly
credits Benjamin Jowett; the other two dialogue captures omit a translation credit.

Retain one canonical record per captured URL, with edition-specific URLs for
separate editions. Preserve copyright notices and terms, and mark truncation.
The project's MIT license covers original project content, not third-party
excerpts, translations, or website material. Capture dates are historical
snapshots, not claims that the source was revised on that date.
